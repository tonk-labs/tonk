# Short invite links, three designs later

Invite URLs embed the whole serialized UCAN chain in `?access=`, which makes them enormous and hostile to chat clients and QR codes. I spent the day designing the replacement and it went through three shapes before landing.

First shape: a capability-gated ==announcement== store. A new `/announcement/publish` effect with `{digest, size, ttl}` params, sealed encrypted envelopes published under the space subject, links as `?invite={subject}/{digest}#{seed}`. We built the whole thing (new `tonk-announcement` crate, service endpoints, mint and claim plumbing, all tested) and it worked. But it needed capability verification on the service, an encryption layer, a new parse format, and claim-side fetch machinery in every client. A lot of apparatus to move one blob.

Second shape: I floated a plain URL shortener with redirects, then it clicked why it can be radically simpler than the announcement design. Two constraints compose: the shortener stores only path + query (a relative `Location` cannot leave the origin, so it is useless as an open redirector), and the stored half of an invite is non-secret by construction (the chain is unredeemable without the seed, and the seed rides the `#fragment`, which browsers never send). Nothing stored is worth protecting, so the service can be ==permissionless==. No UCAN verification, no encryption, no new invite format, and the claim flow in the browser is untouched code: RFC 7231 fragment inheritance carries the seed across the redirect for free.

Final shape, landed in [#586](https://github.com/tonk-labs/tonk/pull/586): `PUT /@?ttl={days}` stores the string under its blake3 hash at `tonk/link/{hash}`, `GET /@/{hash}` answers a relative 301. Links are `hub.tonk.xyz/@/{hash}#{seed}`. Content-addressed keys mean re-publishing is an idempotent expiry refresh and a shortcut can never be repointed. TTL defaults to 7 days, capped at 20, and is expressed in days on purpose: R2 lifecycle rules are day-granular with lazy deletion, so the API should not promise precision the storage cannot keep. Expiry is stamped in object metadata and enforced at read; the lifecycle rule at 21 days just collects the garbage. The announcement build survives on `archive/announcement-invites` if we ever want capability-gated publishing for something else.

> [!caution]
> Two traps in the redirect design. A protocol-relative target (`//evil.tld/x`) is a network-path reference that resolves to another host, so validation has to reject it specifically, not just reject schemes. And the service worker's cache-first app shell would swallow the redirect for anyone who already has the SW installed; `/@` paths get an early return in `onfetch` so the browser talks to the edge directly.

> [!note]
> The shortener also generalizes past invites: share links and agent artifact links are just as enormous, and shortening is a final serialization step over any same-origin URL. One catch, query params on a short link die at the redirect (only the fragment inherits), so share's `name=`/`then=` params must be composed into the target before shortening, not appended after.
