## The button dies, the ceremony signs ahead

Yesterday ended with a "finish backup" button and me asking why it exists at all. Today's answer: it does not. The creation ceremony is the one moment the custody key is in hand, so it now pre-signs the publish invocation, bounded to a month, and hands it back with the sealed cell. The worker drains it on activation, on boot, and when new work queues. No page, no assertion, no button. The cell is also recorded on profile main as a CustodyCell fact, so the account's own sync carries the recovery envelope and the vault copy only bootstraps a brand-new browser.

The old queue comment said an invocation "cannot outlive a wait for an email click" because of its five-minute expiration. We own the expiration. A regression test now pins that a month-old signature still redeems: the service bounds registration ceremonies, not an owner's own cell.

## The dead invite I was told about twice

I reported the share button clean after testing the wrong space. Irakli insisted the consumer was never provisioned, and he was right: one spot carried an attached upstream with no consumer row, share minted link after link for it, and every recipient pulled straight into "the subject is not provisioned". The gate only asked whether an upstream exists. An upstream is not evidence of servability: attachment is durable and replicated through the directory, provisioning lives only server-side, and nothing healed the gap. Now the mint ensures the consumer row first, and since /provider/add is idempotent every share self-heals whatever put the space in that half-state.

Also in that hunt: the Hub's "local" marker is a boot-time overlay meaning "present on this device", it silently missed a space after a service worker restart, and I diagnosed a sync problem from a styling stamp. Cosmetic bug, real confusion, still unfixed.

## The endless loader, actually

Not slow loading. The service worker's shell cache stored the SPA fallback, index.html with a 200, under a hashed asset URL during a rebuild window. Subresource integrity then correctly blocked HTML pretending to be JS, and only a background revalidation healed the entry, which is why refresh usually fixed it. First instinct was to drop SRI; wrong, closed, kept. The right fix teaches the cache that an HTML answer for an asset path is a miss, not content.
