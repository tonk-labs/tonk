# June 9th

## From workspace to a sheet binder

Yesterday's tab-strip work kept tripping over the same thing: a `workspace` that owned a set of sheets and tracked which ones were members. Closing a tab meant retracting a membership fact, and a whole little dance to keep `active` pointing somewhere sane. The more rules I wrote to keep membership honest, the more it looked like I was maintaining bookkeeping for a thing I had invented rather than a thing that exists. The replica already holds the sheets. The workspace was a redundant layer sitting on top of data that was already there.

So I dropped it for a binder. You view a replica as a binder of its sheets. There is no workspace concept owning membership: the sheets are whatever sheets live on the replica, and the binder renders all of them as tabs. A whole class of states disappears with the membership tracking, sheets that exist but are not "in" the workspace, sheets that are in two, membership and sheet disagreeing.

Once membership is gone the only state left is which sheet is active. And the better realization is that `active` was never something to share. Two people on the same replica, or me on a laptop and a phone, do not want their tab selection yoked together. Which sheet I am looking at is per-viewer, per-device, throwaway. Folding it into synced data was always going to feel wrong, and the close-tab focus bugs from yesterday were that wrongness leaking out. So `active` is local selection state on the binder, not a synced fact about the replica.

The model that fell out of this separates `tonk/replica` from a repository. A repository is the storage primitive. A replica is the higher level thing a person actually has access to and views, and `tonk/replica` is the record of it. The binder (`tonk/binder`) binds to a replica and carries the `active` pointer; closing the active sheet moves it to the neighbour the binder supplies, and closing any other sheet leaves it alone because the rule joins on `active: ?sheet` and a non-active close simply does not match. A sheet stays self-describing, its own `entity`/`model`/`view` render directly, so nothing sits between the binder and the content.
