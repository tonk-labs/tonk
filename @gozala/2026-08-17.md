# August 17

## Access metering spec hardening

Spent the day scrutinizing the metering plan ([#702](https://github.com/tonk-labs/tonk/pull/702)) with Claude and rewriting the parts that did not survive it. The architecture held up; the sponsorship math and the registration commands did not.

The sponsor model is now a ==pledge pool==: at the opening of each funding cycle every pledge is withheld from its sponsor's limit and pooled for the consumer, which drains the pool before touching the provider, and gets limited when both are dry until the next cycle re-draws. Shares settle in exact proportion by largest remainder, so every pledge empties at the same moment and unused remainder releases back proportionally, as absence of a charge rather than a refund row. This replaced per-run water-filling with per-sponsor caps, which had a real bug: the available-credit formula subtracted the full pledge and the settled draws, counting the same credits twice.

> [!note]
> The pool forced a latent question into the open: it is one number in credits, so usage has to convert at one plan's rates. The provider's plan is the consumer's service terms; sponsor shares transfer those credits rather than reprice them. The old text claimed per-payer rates, which could never have summed.

> [!caution]
> Periods are per customer and do not align, so "sponsorships are fixed for the cycle" was ill-defined: whose cycle? Now the funding cycle is the provider's billing period, `drawn` is keyed by it, and each sponsor's ledger rows still land in their own period. Two clocks, deliberately never reconciled.

## Role-first commands

Registration commands had `sub: did:web:tonk.network` with the customer as issuer, which no chain can prove. Subjects are customer DIDs now, so the standard chain check covers everything. Commands got renamed role-first, the first segment is the role the subject exercises: `/customer/enroll`, `/provider/add`, `/sponsor/pledge` on a customer DID, `/consumer/provision` as the space-side consent. Prefix authorization then delegates exactly one role, so granting an agent `/provider` cannot leak pledging power.

Consumer add works while the provider is still `Registered`: servability is derived state, so consumers added before the email click just go live with activation, same as the account consumer does.

## Activation is a delegation, not a magic link

The activation email carried a self-signed invocation, a bearer credential in a URL. Now it carries a ==delegation== of `/customer/activate`, audience-bound to the customer, `exp` inside. The customer finalizes by invoking it from the accept button, so history and referrers hold paper only the customer's key can spend, mail-client prefetch is inert, and terms acceptance rides the signed invocation into `terms_version`, evidenced by an artifact the service could not forge. Everything lands at the one `/ucan/` endpoint, the service's own administrative acts included.

Activation lands the customer on a ==trial plan==: generous limits, nothing charged, a `term` the reconciliation cron enforces, successor plans (free tier, paid, maybe card-on-file-no-charge) clear it. Cross-device activation is deposit-for-pickup plus polling, the simple thing first.
