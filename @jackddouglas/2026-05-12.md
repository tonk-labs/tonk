~~If we use `did:plc`, Atproto users get Tonk identity for free and we don't need to worry about identity infra, but it brings me back to my main concerns around Atproto/Bluesky: I don't trust the organization and am pessimistic about it long term, it's not actually a decentralized system since everything points at `plc.directory`, and we're in their wake if their integrity is compromised. The middleground seems to be using `did:plc` for now and designing so we're agnostic to the specific DID method, in case we want something like `did:tonk` in the future.~~

~~One other tension is that I believe PLC favors the user's key for recovery but if we want Tonk to aid in that process for non-technical users then Tonk's own keys would need to be at the top. We can use email for recovery since Tonk will at least operate billing. Users can verify ownership through email and Tonk can co-sign a key rotation.~~

The above over-weights the merkle tree aspect of [[Keybase]]. What Irakli actually wanted to take was the concept of creating full circles between Keybase and other social media integrations. For example, you can post something on X/Twitter and verify on Keybase to prove you control that account. This is a way to build a ledger of online identities you control that others can verify.

Tonk could use this by allowing anyone to bring an existing identity they like (Bluesky/email/etc) and repurpose it for Tonk rather than creating an entirely new one. Billing would work by coupling that identity with Stripe payments, then delegating from the Tonk service to that identity with affordances for paid features.

My points above about Atproto as identity registry still stand, though. It saves us from rebuilding that infra and will help with discoverability, but ties us to an organization and protocol with structural challenges that I don't trust long term, not to mention Tonk getting stuck in the wake of challenges they face upstream. Ideally we'd offer clear offramps into other directories or at least be agnostic to the specific DID method used.

On the question of what to monetize and when: charging or at least offering a way to enter a credit card is a good measure of intent for potential customers. If everything is free, we don't know what actually has legs commercially. It's also a good hedge against enshitification. If we offer to much and charge nothing, it indicates to some people a potential switch-and-bait or inevitable dark patterns for monetization.
