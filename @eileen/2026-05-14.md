## Accounts, Identity, Sharing

Some further decisions with regard to the account and identity system.

- Discussion yesterday about people who get sent something they can write to (perhaps in a somewhat closed group) should not have to sign into an account. Disagreement on what matches existing mental models best, and where we can remove barriers and surface benefits of our architecture.
- Idea today to create "browser profiles" as primitives before accounts - identifiers that are somewhat persistent and tied to your browser (forget Slide for the moment). The name "browser profile" signals that they are attached to your device & browser, rather than your person. Assigning people random (cute) names and avatars helps show that this is a lower-level ID. They can always "upgrade" to a full / more permanent account, where they can edit their display name, picture, and add a social handle. And of course, share repos.
- Question was: do we need them, if people just want to write stuff and don't need to know they have a browser profile? (Similar to "publish repo" flow!)
- (Does this ever break down, if we don't surface them?)

- Ultimately might be a moot point: we share repos the same way, by embedding key generation in the invite link. There are only two kinds of invite links: for readable-only and readable + writable. (This flattens the "publish repo" idea - also no snapshots!) ((Moreover, might make it easier for us to decide what read and write mean in repos with live artifacts.))
- In both cases, we don't need to track anything.
- What we do need to track is *in closed groups* where changes (and authorship) matter. For those groups, we just have people invite each other with their did:keys for now - that's good enough for v1!
- In the future, we can add (1) one-time invitation links and (2) invitation by handle.
